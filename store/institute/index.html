Fundamentals

What is Space Engineering?

Space engineering encompasses the design, analysis, and operation of spacecraft and launch vehicles. It integrates orbital mechanics, propulsion, structures, thermal control, communications, and guidance — all under the extreme constraints of the space environment: vacuum, radiation, microgravity, and vast distances.

Key Concepts

  • Kepler's Laws — (1) orbits are ellipses with the central body at one focus, (2) equal areas in equal times, (3) T^2 is proportional to a^3. Foundation of all orbital mechanics.
  • Orbital Mechanics — the two-body problem, orbital elements (a, e, i, RAAN, omega, nu), Hohmann transfers, bi-elliptic transfers, gravity assists.
  • Rocket Propulsion — Tsiolkovsky's equation: delta-v = Isp * g0 * ln(m0/mf). Chemical (bipropellant, solid), electric (ion, Hall), nuclear thermal.
  • Re-entry — hypersonic aerodynamics, thermal protection (ablative, ceramic tiles), ballistic vs lifting entry, skip entry. Heat flux scales as v^3.
  • Orbital Maneuvers — Hohmann transfer (minimum energy), plane changes, rendezvous and docking, station-keeping, de-orbit burns.
  • Attitude Control — reaction wheels, control moment gyroscopes, thrusters, magnetic torquers. Quaternion/Euler angle representations.

Space vs Aviation

  • No air: no aerodynamic lift or drag in orbit. All maneuvers use propellant (finite delta-v budget).
  • Orbital speed: ~7.8 km/s in LEO. You don't "hover" in space — you fall around the Earth in a continuous free-fall trajectory.
  • Communication delays: 1.3s to Moon, 3-22 min to Mars, hours to outer planets. Spacecraft must be increasingly autonomous.
  • Radiation: Van Allen belts, solar particle events, galactic cosmic rays. Electronics need radiation hardening; crews need shielding.

Textbooks

  • Curtis: Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students — Best intro to orbital mechanics. Two-body problem, orbit determination, interplanetary flight, rigid body dynamics.
  • Wertz: Space Mission Engineering — The New SMAD — THE systems engineering reference for space missions. Architecture, budgets, subsystems, cost estimation.
  • Sutton & Biblarz: Rocket Propulsion Elements — Definitive textbook on rocket propulsion. Nozzle theory, liquid/solid/hybrid/electric propulsion, performance analysis.
  • Battin: Astrodynamics — Graduate-level mathematical astrodynamics. Lambert's problem, perturbation theory, universal variables. The rigorous reference.
  • Fortescue et al: Spacecraft Systems Engineering — Comprehensive spacecraft design. Structures, thermal, power, OBDH, telecommunications, AOCS, propulsion.

Key Research Papers

Tsiolkovsky (1903): Rocket Equation

Derived the fundamental equation of rocketry: delta-v = ve * ln(m0/mf). Proved spaceflight was physically possible. The foundation of all astronautics.

Konstantin Tsiolkovsky | Russia

Hohmann (1925): The Attainability of Heavenly Bodies

Proposed the minimum-energy transfer orbit between two circular orbits (Hohmann transfer). Still the most fuel-efficient two-impulse maneuver.

Walter Hohmann | NASA translation

Parkinson & Spilker (1996): GPS Overview

Comprehensive overview of the Global Positioning System. Satellite constellation design, signal structure, navigation algorithms, error sources.

GPS: Theory & Applications | AIAA

Apollo Guidance Computer (1967)

Documentation of the AGC that guided Apollo missions. 74KB ROM, 4KB RAM, real-time navigation. A masterpiece of early embedded systems engineering.

MIT Instrumentation Lab | NASA

Kessler & Cour-Palais (1978): Collision Frequency

Predicted cascading orbital debris collisions (Kessler Syndrome). Collisions create fragments that cause more collisions, potentially making orbits unusable.

Donald Kessler | NASA JSC

Edelbaum (1961): Low-Thrust Transfer

Optimal low-thrust orbit transfer theory. Foundation for electric propulsion trajectory design used in modern ion-engine missions.

Theodore Edelbaum | AIAA Journal

Reference

Orbital Mechanics

  • Kepler's Laws — (1) elliptical orbits, (2) equal areas swept in equal times (conservation of angular momentum), (3) period squared proportional to semi-major axis cubed: T^2 = (4 pi^2 / mu) * a^3
  • Vis-viva Equation — v^2 = mu * (2/r - 1/a). Relates speed to position and orbit size. Works for all conics (circles, ellipses, parabolas, hyperbolas).
  • Hohmann Transfer — minimum-energy two-impulse transfer between circular orbits. Delta-v at periapsis and apoapsis. For LEO to GEO: ~3.9 km/s total.
  • Orbital Elements — six Keplerian elements: semi-major axis (a), eccentricity (e), inclination (i), RAAN (Omega), argument of periapsis (omega), true anomaly (nu).

Propulsion

  • Tsiolkovsky Equation — delta-v = Isp * g0 * ln(m0/mf). Higher Isp = more efficient. Chemical Isp: 200-460s. Electric Isp: 1000-5000s.
  • Chemical Propulsion — bipropellant (LOX/LH2, LOX/RP-1, NTO/MMH), solid (ammonium perchlorate + aluminum), hybrid.
  • Electric Propulsion — ion (Xe gridded), Hall effect, pulsed plasma. High Isp but low thrust. Used for station-keeping and deep space.
  • Staging — discard empty tanks to reduce mass. Tsiolkovsky equation applied per stage. Optimal mass ratios. Serial vs parallel staging.

Spacecraft Subsystems

  • ADCS — Attitude Determination and Control. Sensors (star trackers, sun sensors, IMUs), actuators (reaction wheels, CMGs, thrusters, magnetorquers).
  • Power — solar panels (GaAs triple-junction, ~30% efficiency), batteries (Li-ion), RTGs for deep space (Pu-238). Power budget: generation, storage, distribution.
  • Thermal — passive (MLI blankets, radiators, coatings) and active (heaters, heat pipes, louvers). Space: -270C shadow to +120C sun.
  • Communications — link budget: transmit power + antenna gain - path loss - noise. S/X/Ka bands. DSN for deep space. Laser comms emerging.
